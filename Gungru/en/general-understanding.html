☸ About Tibetan Buddhism

What is Tibetan Buddhism? +
Tibetan Buddhism is a form of Mahayana Buddhism that developed in Tibet and the Himalayan regions. It combines the teachings of the historical Buddha with tantric practices, philosophical study, and devotional rituals. It emphasizes compassion for all sentient beings, the pursuit of wisdom, and the aspiration to achieve enlightenment for the benefit of others.
What is the Gelug tradition? +
The Gelug school, founded by Je Tsongkhapa in the early 15th century, is one of four major schools of Tibetan Buddhism. It emphasizes rigorous philosophical study, monastic discipline, and the gradual path to enlightenment. Gaden Monastery, established by Tsongkhapa himself in 1409, is the principal monastery of the Gelug tradition. His Holiness the Dalai Lama is the most well-known teacher of the Gelug lineage.
What is a Khangtsen? +
A Khangtsen (also spelled Khangsten) is a regional house within a larger Tibetan monastery. Monks are organized into Khangtsens based on their geographic origin in Tibet. Gungru Khangtsen is one of eleven such houses within Gaden Shartse Monastery. Each Khangtsen provides housing, meals, and community support for its monks, functioning as a close-knit family within the larger monastic institution.
What are the Four Noble Truths? +
The Four Noble Truths are the foundational teaching of Buddhism, first taught by the Buddha after his enlightenment. They are: (1) the truth of suffering — that life inevitably involves dissatisfaction, (2) the truth of the origin of suffering — that suffering arises from craving and ignorance, (3) the truth of the cessation of suffering — that it is possible to end suffering, and (4) the truth of the path — that there is a practical path leading to the end of suffering, known as the Noble Eightfold Path.

🙏 About Pujas & Prayers

What is a Puja? +
A Puja is a Buddhist prayer ceremony performed by monks. It involves chanting sacred texts, making offerings, and performing rituals directed toward specific purposes such as healing, protection, purification, or generating merit. Pujas can be requested by individuals for themselves or on behalf of others, including loved ones who have passed away.
What types of Pujas can I request? +
We offer several types of Pujas including: Medicine Buddha Puja for healing and health, Tara Puja for protection and removing obstacles, Manjushri Puja for wisdom and academic success, Lama Choepa (Guru Puja) for spiritual growth, and special prayers for the deceased to guide them toward a favorable rebirth. Contact us to discuss which Puja is most appropriate for your needs.
How do I request a Puja? +
You can request a Puja by contacting us through our website or via email at gadengungru@gmail.com. We will discuss your needs and intentions, and our monks will perform the ceremony at the monastery in India. You will receive confirmation and blessings after the Puja is completed.
What is a Butter Lamp offering? +
A butter lamp is a traditional Tibetan Buddhist offering. The light of the lamp symbolizes wisdom dispelling the darkness of ignorance. You can sponsor a butter lamp to be lit in your name or in memory of a loved one. It is one of the simplest and most meaningful offerings you can make, and is available for a small donation of ₹108 (~$1.30 USD).

🏯 About the Monastery

Where is Gaden Shartse Gungru Khangtsen located? +
The monastery is located in Mundgod, Karnataka, India. After the Chinese invasion of Tibet in 1959 and the subsequent exile of His Holiness the Dalai Lama, the Tibetan monastic community was re-established in South India. Mundgod is home to one of the largest Tibetan settlements in India, housing several major monasteries including Gaden Shartse and Gaden Jangtse.
What do the monks study? +
Monks undertake an intensive program of Buddhist philosophical studies that typically spans 15–20 years. The curriculum includes the Five Great Treatises: Pramana (logic and epistemology), Prajnaparamita (perfection of wisdom), Madhyamaka (middle way philosophy), Abhidharma (metaphysics), and Vinaya (monastic discipline). Study is conducted primarily through rigorous debate, a distinctive hallmark of the Gelug tradition.
How can I visit the monastery? +
Visitors are welcome at the monastery in Mundgod. The nearest major airport is Hubli (HBX), approximately 1.5 hours away. We recommend contacting us in advance at gadengungru@gmail.com so we can help arrange your visit and ensure monks are available to welcome you and show you around the monastery.
How can I support the monastery? +
There are many ways to support us: making a financial donation, sponsoring a monk's meals or education, requesting a Puja, or simply spreading awareness about our work. Every contribution, no matter the size, makes a meaningful difference in the lives of our monks.

🎨 About Sand Mandalas

What is a Sand Mandala? +
A sand mandala is an intricate design created from millions of grains of colored sand. Monks spend days or weeks carefully placing each grain using traditional metal funnels called chak-pur to create a sacred geometric pattern representing the Buddhist universe or the palace of a specific deity. The creation of a sand mandala is itself a form of meditation and prayer.
Why is the Sand Mandala destroyed after completion? +
The ritual dissolution of a sand mandala is one of its most powerful teachings. After days of painstaking creation, the mandala is ceremonially swept away, symbolizing the Buddhist teaching of impermanence — that all things, no matter how beautiful, are transient. The sand is then distributed to attendees as a blessing, and a portion is traditionally released into a nearby body of water to spread the blessings to all beings.
Where can I see a Sand Mandala being created? +
Our monks create sand mandalas at the monastery and at special events. Follow us on social media or contact us at gadengungru@gmail.com to learn about upcoming events near you.

💖 Donations & Sponsorship

Where does my donation go? +
Every donation directly supports the monastic community. Funds are used for daily meals for over 30 monks, educational materials (textbooks, debate manuals, and library resources), healthcare (especially for elderly monks), building maintenance of the prayer hall, dormitories, and kitchen, and ceremony supplies for pujas and offerings. We are a small community, and every contribution makes a tangible difference.
Can I sponsor a single monk? +
Yes! Our monk sponsorship program allows you to support an individual monk’s meals, education, and basic needs on a monthly basis. Sponsoring a monk costs approximately ₹3,000 / $35 USD per month and covers food, study materials, and healthcare. Contact us at gadengungru@gmail.com and we will match you with a monk and share updates on their progress.
Is my donation tax-deductible? +
For Indian donors, donations are eligible for tax exemption under Section 80G of the Income Tax Act. We can provide an 80G certificate upon request. For international donors, tax deductibility depends on your country’s laws. We provide a receipt for all donations, which you may be able to use for tax purposes. Please consult your local tax advisor.
What currencies do you accept? +
We accept donations in INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AUD, CAD, SGD, HKD, and many other currencies through our secure online payment system. You can also donate via bank transfer (NEFT/IMPS for Indian donors) or UPI. Visit our Donate page for all available payment methods.
Can I set up a recurring monthly donation? +
Yes! Our Donate page offers a monthly recurring option. Simply select “Monthly” when making your donation. You can cancel at any time. Recurring donations are especially valuable because they help us plan ahead and provide consistent support for the monks’ daily needs.
How do I know my donation was received? +
You will receive a confirmation email with a receipt as soon as your donation is processed. If you opted in, we also send periodic updates about how your contributions have supported the monastery. If you don’t receive a confirmation within 24 hours, please check your spam folder or contact us at gadengungru@gmail.com.

🌃 Visiting the Monastery

When is the best time to visit? +
The best time to visit is October through March, when the weather in South India is cooler and more comfortable. The monsoon season (June–September) brings heavy rain and can make travel difficult. If you time your visit during major Buddhist holidays — such as Losar (Tibetan New Year, Feb/Mar), Saga Dawa (May/Jun), or the annual Monlam Prayer Festival — you can witness special ceremonies and celebrations.
How do I get to Mundgod? +
Mundgod is located in the Uttara Kannada district of Karnataka, India. The nearest airport is Hubli (HBX), approximately 1.5 hours by road. Other options include Goa (GOI) airport (~4 hours) and Bangalore (BLR) (~8 hours). From Hubli, you can hire a taxi or take a local bus to Mundgod. We can help arrange transportation if you contact us in advance.
Is there accommodation near the monastery? +
Yes, there are guesthouses within the Tibetan settlement in Mundgod that offer basic but comfortable rooms. Some visitors also stay in nearby towns such as Hubli, which has a wider range of hotels. Contact us at gadengungru@gmail.com and we can recommend accommodation and help coordinate your stay.
What should I wear and how should I behave? +
Please wear modest clothing that covers your shoulders and knees. When entering the prayer hall, remove your shoes and keep your phone on silent. Photography is generally welcome in public areas but please ask permission before photographing monks or ceremonies. Visitors should maintain a respectful and quiet demeanor, especially during prayer sessions. The monks are friendly and happy to answer questions outside of prayer times.
Can I attend prayer ceremonies? +
Yes, visitors are welcome to attend daily morning and evening prayer sessions in the prayer hall. You may sit quietly at the back and observe. There is no requirement to participate in the chanting or prostrations — simply being present with a respectful attitude is appreciated. Special pujas and ceremonies may also be open to visitors; ask a monk or contact us for the schedule.
Do I need to arrange my visit in advance? +
We strongly recommend contacting us before your visit by emailing gadengungru@gmail.com. This allows us to ensure monks are available to welcome you, arrange a guided tour of the monastery, and help with accommodation suggestions. Walk-in visitors are welcome, but advance notice means we can offer you a much richer experience.

🕔 Puja Requests — Practical Details

How long before my puja is performed? +
Most pujas are performed within 1–2 weeks of receiving your request. The timing depends on the monastery’s prayer schedule and the type of puja requested. For urgent matters such as serious illness or the passing of a loved one, we can arrange prayers within a few days. If you have a specific date in mind, please mention it in your request and we will do our best to accommodate it.
How much does a puja cost? +
There is no fixed price for pujas. Prayer ceremonies are offered freely to anyone who requests them, regardless of ability to donate. However, suggested offerings typically range from ₹500–₹5,000 (approximately $6–$60 USD) depending on the type and duration of the ceremony. Donations help cover the cost of offerings, butter lamps, and materials used during the puja, and support the monks’ livelihood.
Can I request a puja for someone who has passed away? +
Yes, prayers for the deceased are one of the most common and important requests we receive. In Tibetan Buddhism, prayers and merit generated in the 49 days following death are believed to be especially powerful in guiding the consciousness toward a favorable rebirth. We perform dedicated prayers, butter lamp offerings, and merit dedication ceremonies for the departed. You can request these at any time, even after the 49-day period.
Will I receive confirmation after the puja? +
Yes. After the puja is performed, you will receive an email confirmation with the date and details of the ceremony. When possible, we also include a photo of the offerings and prayer hall. The email will include a blessing message from the monks, which you can share with the person the puja was dedicated to.
Can I request a puja from outside India? +
Absolutely. The majority of our puja requests come from people around the world. Our monks perform all ceremonies at the monastery in Mundgod, India, and the blessings are not limited by physical distance. You can submit your request through our Request a Puja page or by emailing gadengungru@gmail.com.
What information do you need for my puja request? +
To arrange your puja, we need: (1) the full name of the person the puja is for, (2) the type of puja you are requesting, (3) the intention or reason (e.g., healing, protection, merit for the deceased), and (4) a preferred date if you have one (optional). You can submit all of this through our online form or email us directly.
